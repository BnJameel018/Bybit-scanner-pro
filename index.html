How the rules are read
  1. Timeframes. D1 mode: rejection on D1, resistance break on H4, swing-high close on H1. W1 mode: rejection on W1, resistance break on D1, swing-high close on H4. The last closed candle on the rejection timeframe and the one still forming are checked for a rejection.
  2. Support: a bearish candle followed by a bullish one, at the bearish candle's close. Touches count from the candle after the bullish one. A resistance (bullish candle then bearish, at the bullish close) that a candle has closed above also becomes a support, with touches counted after that closing candle.
  3. A close below a support does not end it if a later, different candle closes back above it. That break and reclaim can happen once. A second close below ends it. A flipped resistance gets no reclaim: its first close below ends it for good.
  4. Rejection: the last closed candle is the first to touch the support (low at or under it) after the reclaim, if there was one, and it closes above it.
  5. Resistance: a bullish then bearish candle on the break timeframe, at the bullish close, above the support, completed before the first touch and never closed above before it. The lowest one is used.
  6. Trigger: a break-timeframe close above the resistance, and a swing-high-timeframe close above the swing high, in either order. The swing high is the high of the most recent valid correction in the bearish move into the support: a bounce that trades above the high of the last impulse candle and is complete once price makes a new lower low (swings inside it are ignored). If price fell in one straight impulse, it is the high of the candle before the fall. It can sit below the resistance.
  7. Exception: if a later break-timeframe candle goes below the first touch's low and closes above the support, the highest unbroken resistance formed after the first touch replaces the original and follows the same trigger rule.
  8. A pair is dropped when, at scan time, price has reached the lowest valid resistance on the rejection timeframe since the first touch (the still-forming break-timeframe candle counts), unless a break-timeframe candle has closed above that resistance since the first touch (H4 for a daily resistance, D1 for a weekly one).
  9. LHR (left hand): the support belongs to a different move from the fall that rejects at it. Read on the swing-high timeframe: the highest high between the support's bearish candle and the first touch must come after that candle, meaning price rallied away from the support and fell back. Flipped resistances are not tagged, since they are always left hands. CRT: the rejection candle sweeps below the low of an earlier candle and closes back inside that candle's range, stays under its high, and no candle since has traded below that low or above that high.
  10. Only triggered setups are listed. Incomplete ones are saved on this phone and rechecked on each scan, and appear once they trigger. The rejection candle may still be forming on the rejection timeframe; its price so far counts as its close, and the setup disappears on a later scan if that candle closes below the support. Triggered setups stay listed for 3 days (14 days in W1 mode).
